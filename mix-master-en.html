01

Send Your Material

The process begins with you sending the material. Along with the multitracks and the rough mix (demo), you also share references, information about the song, and your expectations for the final result. This is also the time to tell me what you already love about the track and, more importantly, what you still feel is missing. The more context I have at this stage, the more productive our email discussion will be.

02

Email Discussion

After reviewing everything you sent, we continue the conversation by email. This step is essential to align my perception of the project and the references you shared with your artistic vision. If I identify any issue that requires deeper correction—such as recording, editing, or other technical problems that could affect the final result—I will explain what needs to be addressed before the mixing and mastering process begins. This exchange ensures we share the same goals before moving forward.

03

Mixing & Mastering

From this point on, every decision follows what we discussed. My goal is simple: to translate the direction we defined together into sound. Unless you specifically want otherwise, my intention is never to leave my signature on your music, but to bring out the very best it already has.

04

First Delivery

You receive the first version and listen carefully, taking your time. I recommend listening on as many systems as possible (TV, phone, headphones, car, etc.) over at least one day. Take notes on what worked and what still does not sound as you imagined. Honest feedback is an essential part of the process.

05

Revisions

Up to three revision rounds are included. Their purpose is to refine the artistic direction we have already established, not to make endless changes. Each round brings the track closer to your vision.

06

Final Delivery

Once the revisions are complete, you receive the final files ready for any platform. If you'd like, we can wrap up with a final email exchange—not to sell you anything else, but simply to hear how the experience was from your perspective.
